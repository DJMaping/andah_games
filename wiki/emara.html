Emara officially the Emaran Republic located primarily in South Ayuma. Its overseas regions and territories include Tiesa Canal territory, Eldas Isle, Weimrino, and Lyters in the Hinsakian Ocean, and many islands in South and North Eanif Ocean, giving it one of the largest discontinuous exclusive economic zones in the world. Emara shares borders with Siana and Lycroa to the north; also borders Trian and Etretes via the Tiesa canal territory, and has a maritime border with Easuhura to the east. Its metropolitan area extends from the Druvain river to the north, Vihsanna Ocean, and from the Rianian Sea to the Escaran Channel. Its 32 central regions—1 of which is overseas—span a combined area of 428,786 km2 (165,555 sq mi) and have an estimated total population of over 90.8 million as of 1765. Emara is a semi-presidential republic and its capital, largest city, and main cultural and economic center is Rivostan.

Emara was settled during the Bronze age by Carth tribes known as Arznx before Ecossians first started settling the east coast in the 5-4th centuries BFA, followed by the conquests by the Vestorian empire in the 1st century BFA, leading to a distinct Vestor-Ecossian culture. In the Early Middle Ages, the Emarans formed the kingdom of Destria, which became the heartland of the Destrain Empire. The Treaty of Abutot in 664 partitioned the empire, with West Bervia evolving into the Kingdom of Poime. In the Early Middle Ages, Emara was a powerful but decentralized feudal kingdom, but from the mid-11th to the mid-12th centuries, Emara was plunged into a dynastic conflict with Easuhura known as the Two Hundred Years' War. In the 14th century, Emaran culture flourished during the Emaran Renaissance under Lycoan rule, and an Emaran colonial empire emerged. Internally, Emara was dominated by the conflict with the Lycroans under the Friliens and the growing religious conflict between Inurraconas and Lengea. Emara was successful in the Redea Succession war and further increased its influence during the reign of Zonasa the Honourable.

The Emaran Revolution of 1577 overthrew the Dialxao Regime and produced the Declaration of the Rights of All, which continues to express the nation's ideals to this day. Emara reached its political and military zenith in the early 17th century under the newly formed Liberal party, becoming the primary force behind Ayuman imperialism and establishing the First Emaran Empire. The collapse of the empire initiated a period of relative decline, in which Emara endured the Sicla Restoration until the founding of the Emaran Second Republic, which was succeeded by the Third Republic upon the 1644 Revolts. Emara was one of the major participants of The Great Imperial War, from which it emerged victorious at great human and economic cost. It was among the Victors of the Red war, but it was occupied for most of the war. Following its liberation, the short-lived Sixth Republic was established and later dissolved after military defeats in Haiza. The current Seventh Republic was formed in 1710. Most Emaran colonies became independent in the 1700s, with the majority retaining close economic and military ties with Emara. Migoku voted to stay under the jurisdiction of Emara, but the election was later shown to be fraudulent.

Emara retains its centuries-long status as a global center of art, science, and philosophy. It hosts the sixth-largest number of Global Heritage Sites and is the world's 2nd-leading tourist destination, having received 120 million foreign visitors in 1763. A developed country, Emara has a high nominal per capita income globally, and its advanced economy ranks among the largest in the world by both nominal GDP and. It is a great power, having one of the largest militaries and an official nuclear-weapon state. The country is part of multiple international organisations and forums.
